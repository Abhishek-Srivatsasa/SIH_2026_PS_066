The blind spot

Satellites see the skin. Heat lives below.

Ocean heat content, cyclone intensification, marine heatwaves and fisheries all depend on what happens in the top few hundred metres. Yet Earth observation only reads the surface, and Argo floats sample sparsely in space and time. The Indian Ocean — home to a population of 1.4 billion — has the largest such blind spot in the world.

The challenge

Only SST, SSS, sea-surface height and currents are measured globally. The 3-D thermal structure beneath — the part that drives cyclones — is invisible to orbiters.

Our approach

A U-Net convolution encoder–decoder learns the surface→subsurface mapping and emits all 15 standard depth levels in one forward pass — an operational-grade virtual sensor.

Architecture

Five surface fields in. Fifteen depths out.

A compact U-Net: two downsampling stages, a 128-channel latent embedding, and a symmetric decoder re-fused by skip connections — with a vertical-consistency penalty in the loss.

Surface inputs Encoder Latent embedding Decoder Depth outputs

Loss with vertical memory

A masked MSE keeps land and seafloor cells out of the gradient, while a gradient penalty forces the reconstructed profiles to stay vertically coherent between neighbouring depths.

Skip-connected decoder

Transposed convolutions upsample the 128-channel latent field, and each decoder stage re-fuses the matching encoder feature map — preserving fine mesoscale fronts that pooling alone would blur away.

Train-only normalisation

Every channel is standardised with statistics computed only on January–April. Nothing about the May–June holdout ever leaks into the model.

Honest scope

What this prototype is — and is not

Hackathon work earns trust when its edges are drawn clearly. So here are ours, on the record.

Demonstrated today

  • A U-Net CNN mapping 5 surface fields to 15 subsurface depth levels.
  • Inputs derived from GLORYS12V1 reanalysis, coarsened from 1/12° to 0.25°.
  • Daily coverage, Jan–Jun 2021, over an 80–90°E / 10–20°N Bay of Bengal box.
  • 120 training days and 61 strictly held-out test days.
  • Benchmarked against a January–April temporal-mean baseline at every cell and depth.

Not yet claimed

  • Not a Vision Transformer architecture.
  • Not conditioned on geographic basin or salinity-derived stratification.
  • Not yet validated against in-situ ARGO float profiles.
  • Not covering the Arabian Sea or the full North Indian Ocean domain.
  • Not an operational near-real-time forecasting system.

What comes next

  1. Next

    Real satellite inputs

    Move from reanalysis surface fields to OSTIA SST, SMAP/SMOS SSS, DUACS SSH, OSCAR currents and CCMP/ASCAT wind stress.

  2. Then

    In-situ ARGO validation

    Direct verification against independent ARGO profiles from the INCOIS Live Access Server.

  3. Then

    Domain expansion

    Broaden to the full North Indian Ocean, 5°N–30°N and 45°E–105°E, with basin conditioning.

  4. Scale

    ViT benchmark & service

    Benchmark spatial-attention transformers against the CNN backbone and expose a near-real-time inference API.
